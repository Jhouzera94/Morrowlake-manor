The player wakes beside a bed or broken time machine in Morrowlake Manor, confused and alone.
A tape recorder nearby says something like: “If you woke up, the machine failed. Restore the power. Find the plans. Do not trust what the house tells you.”
They find the first blueprint and add it to Evidence. It reveals the next area to search.
Each major item leads naturally to another:Manor key → opens a locked room
Blueprint → shows a hidden route or generator house
Missing fuse → repairs the generator
Generator power → activates lights, doors, and the tower connection
Final machine component/key → lets them start the time machine

Once the player activates the machine, they travel back once—to the same manor on the night of the disaster.
In the past, the house is alive: lights work, rooms are furnished, tapes/letters can be understood in context, and the player discovers what happened to the owner, the manor, and the machine.
The final revelation explains why the player woke there and what they must do before returning—or whether they should return at all.
That gives you a very clear dissertation point: the architecture tells the story through access and discovery. The player understands the manor first as a damaged, abandoned space; then revisits it in the past and understands the meaning of each room, object, and route.
