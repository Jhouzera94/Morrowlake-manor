I’d keep the ending short, emotional, and slightly mysterious. The player should understand enough to feel rewarded without introducing another large mission.

## Simple story idea: “The Night Morrowlake Disappeared”

In 1950, Dr. Elias Morrowlake was building the time machine beneath the manor. He believed he could use it to prevent the death of his daughter, Evelyn.

The machine malfunctioned during its first test. The energy tower overloaded, everyone evacuated, and the manor became trapped in a strange temporal disturbance. The abandoned present-day mansion is the result.

Throughout the modern manor, the player repairs the same systems Elias used: the generator, energy tower, cable, handheld calibration reader, and portal.

### Clues to leave around the modern manor

Use only five or six important clues:

1. **Family photograph**

   Elias, Evelyn, and the rest of the family standing outside the manor. Evelyn’s face is marked with a small handwritten circle.

2. **Doctor’s note**

   “Her condition is worsening. The doctors say she will not survive the winter.”

3. **Workshop journal**

   “Time is not a river. It is a door—and I intend to open it.”

4. **Warning from an assistant**

   “Elias, the tower cannot sustain another test. If you activate it again, evacuate the manor.”

5. **Damaged newspaper**

   “Morrowlake Manor Abandoned After Mysterious Electrical Fire — 1950.”

6. **Final recorded message near the portal**

   “If you find this machine, do not come to save me. Find Evelyn. Tell her that none of this was her fault.”

These clues explain the story without long text. Each note could be two or three sentences.

## What happens after travelling to 1950

The player arrives inside the same tube, but the manor is clean, warm, and inhabited. It is nighttime, and alarms are sounding. The energy tower can be seen flashing through a window.

The player leaves the tube and walks down one short corridor. They meet **Evelyn Morrowlake**, around 18–20 years old, standing near the laboratory door.

She is the only NPC needed.

### Short interaction

Evelyn asks:

> “You came through my father’s machine, didn’t you?”

The player can choose one of two simple replies:

- “Your father sent me.”
- “The machine destroys this place.”

Both lead to the same ending.

Evelyn explains that her father is currently preparing the machine’s first full-power test. She now understands that his attempt to save her will destroy the manor.

She gives the player a choice:

- **Stop the test**
- **Let history happen**

For a very small ending, the choice can happen entirely through dialogue.

## Recommended ending

The player chooses **Stop the test**.

Evelyn takes the handheld calibration reader that the player installed and says:

> “Then I know what I have to do.”

She runs toward the laboratory. The lights fail. There is a loud electrical surge, followed by silence.

The screen fades to black.

Then the player wakes outside Morrowlake Manor in the present day. The building is no longer abandoned—it is restored, peaceful, and occupied.

The family photograph appears one final time. Elias and Evelyn are both present, but the player is now faintly visible in the background.

Final text:

> “History remembered someone who was never meant to be there.”

Credits.

## Even simpler ending

If restoring the modern manor is too much work, end while still in 1950:

Evelyn says:

> “If you’re here, then his machine worked. And if it worked… something terrible must have happened.”

An alarm begins.

She looks toward the laboratory and says:

> “Help me stop him.”

The player follows her toward the door. Before they reach it, the screen fades to black.

Final text:

> “The future of Morrowlake Manor has changed.”

This version needs only one NPC, one corridor, a short conversation, lighting effects, and a fade to credits. It leaves room for a sequel without requiring another gameplay section.
